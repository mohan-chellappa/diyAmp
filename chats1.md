No, not directly on its own. An ESP32 cannot natively log into YouTube or stream directly from YouTube Music over Wi-Fi.

Here is why, and more importantly, how you actually solve it for your family:

Why the ESP32 Cannot Stream YouTube Directly
Google’s DRM & Complex Protocol: YouTube and YouTube Music do not have an open stream link. They use complex, encrypted HTTPS chunking (DASH/HLS) and constantly changing signature algorithms that require massive computing power and frequent updates to decode.
No "YouTube Connect": Unlike Spotify (which has Spotify Connect allowing embedded chips to stream independently), Google does not offer an open equivalent for YouTube Music.
Hardware Limits: An ESP32 has only a few megabytes of RAM and a 240MHz microcontroller chip — it cannot run the heavy browser engine or Google security certificates needed to play YouTube directly.
How Your Family Will Play YouTube / YouTube Music (2 Easy Ways)
You don't need the ESP32 to connect to YouTube directly because of these two rock-solid methods:

Method 1: Bluetooth A2DP Sink (The Phone Route)
This is what 99% of people do, and it works seamlessly:

Any family member opens YouTube or YouTube Music on their phone, tablet, or laptop.
They tap Connect Bluetooth to the Amp.
The ESP32 acts as a Bluetooth Audio Receiver and feeds clean digital audio (I2S) directly into your DAC.
The Magic Feature: Even over Bluetooth, the ESP32 can extract AVRCP Metadata — meaning the Song Title and Artist Name from YouTube Music will still display on your screen, and your EC11 knob can pause/skip tracks on their phone!
Method 2: The Smart TV Route
Most families watch YouTube and YouTube Music videos on the Smart TV in the living room:

Connect your Smart TV's Optical (TOSLINK) output to a DAC board in your amplifier.
When they play any music video on YouTube on the TV, it automatically blasts through your Hi-Fi speakers.
What the ESP32 Can Stream Directly (Without Any Phone)
If you want the amp to play music independently over Wi-Fi without needing a phone:

Thousands of Internet Radio Stations: Streams live MP3/AAC streams directly (BBC, FM Gold, Radio Paradise, Jazz, Devotional stations) with one push of the knob.
Spotify Connect: Through open-source firmware (like Squeezelite-ESP32), an ESP32 can stream Spotify tracks directly from Wi-Fi.
Local Home Server (DLNA / UPnP): Streams music stored on a home PC or NAS drive.
Summary
To play YouTube / YouTube Music, your family will simply use their phone via Bluetooth or the Smart TV via Optical. The ESP32 will handle the audio conversion, show the song title on the screen, and let the physical knob control volume and track playback.

To connect your mobile phone and the ESP32 amplifier with two-way data flow, there are two primary methods.

The best design uses both together:

                       ┌────────────────────────┐
                       │   Mobile Phone         │
                       │   (iOS / Android)      │
                       └────┬──────────────┬────┘
                            │              │
        Music Audio &       │              │  Two-Way Control Data
        Basic Track Info    │              │  (Volume, EQ, Inputs, Status)
        (A2DP + AVRCP)      │              │  (WebSockets / Wi-Fi)
                            ▼              ▼
                       ┌────────────────────────┐
                       │    ESP32 Amplifier     │
                       │  (5" Screen + Knob)    │
                       └────────────────────────┘
Method 1: The "Zero-App" Native Route (Bluetooth AVRCP)
Best for: Everyday music playback without installing any app on anyone's phone.

When a phone connects via standard Bluetooth audio, it uses two profiles:

A2DP: Streams the raw music audio from phone to amp.
AVRCP (Audio/Video Remote Control Profile): A built-in, two-way data channel supported natively by iOS and Android.
Data Flow:
Phone ➔ ESP32: The phone automatically pushes metadata to the ESP32:
Song Title, Artist Name, Album Name
Track duration and playback state (Playing / Paused)
Phone volume level
ESP32 ➔ Phone:
Turning your EC11 knob sends volume increments back to the phone.
Clicking the knob sends PLAY / PAUSE / NEXT commands to Spotify, YouTube Music, or Apple Music on the phone.
Method 2: The Dedicated Web App (Wi-Fi + WebSockets)
Best for: Full remote control (EQ, input selection, diagnostics, radio presets).

Instead of building and publishing an app for the Google Play Store or Apple App Store (which requires developer accounts and native code), the ESP32 hosts its own local web server on your home Wi-Fi network.

Any family member opens http://amp.local on their phone browser (and can tap "Add to Home Screen" so it looks and behaves exactly like a native app).

How Two-Way Data Flow Works (WebSockets):
A persistent, bi-directional WebSocket connection stays open between the phone and the ESP32:

Phone ➔ ESP32:
Slide volume bar on phone ➔ ESP32 instantly adjusts volume and updates the 5" screen.
Tap "TV Optical" or "Bluetooth" ➔ ESP32 switches the audio relays/multiplexer.
Adjust Bass / Treble / EQ ➔ ESP32 updates the DSP/DAC tone registers.
ESP32 ➔ Phone:
You turn the physical EC11 knob on the front panel ➔ The volume slider on the phone moves in real time.
The amp detects an event (e.g., heatsink temperature, clipping warning, input change) ➔ The phone UI updates immediately.
Why this fits clean architecture:
Tech Stack: Vanilla JavaScript + W3.CSS served directly from the ESP32’s flash (SPIFFS / LittleFS).
Payloads: Compact JSON or 2D array data packets sent over the WebSocket (e.g., ["VOL", 42], ["SRC", "OPT1"], ["TEMP", 38.5]).
Zero Dependencies: No Node.js, no external cloud servers, and no app store maintenance.
Recommended Implementation Strategy
Feature	Handled By	How it Works
Play/Pause, Track Skip, Now Playing Info	Bluetooth AVRCP	Works automatically with Spotify / YouTube Music / Apple Music without launching any browser or app.
Full Remote Control & Settings	Local Web App (amp.local)	Open on any phone/tablet/laptop to tweak EQ, select inputs, configure Wi-Fi, or monitor amplifier health.
